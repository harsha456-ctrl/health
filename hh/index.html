Project Core Objective & Research Gap

Problem Statement

Design an explainable, IoT-deployable early-warning system for heart failure mortality risk using patient continuous monitoring data. The system must predict risk in real time and explain why a patient is flagged high-risk directly on low-power edge devices.

Resolved Gaps vs Existing Literature

  • Offline vs Real-Time: Existing works (Chicco 2020, Ishaq 2021) used static offline datasets. We simulate continuous IoT streams.
  • Cloud Dependency: Umer 2022 relied on cloud servers. Our system runs edge inference with latency < 1 ms.
  • Explainability: We embed SHAP values to explain individual patient risk triggers.

Benchmark Insights & Analysis

Matthews Correlation (MCC)

Standard accuracy can be misleading due to class imbalance (203 survived vs 96 deceased). Chicco & Jurman emphasized MCC. Our LightGBM model achieves 0.6486 MCC in cross-validation.

Edge Feasibility

With average per-sample inference latency of 0.05ms to 0.55ms, our model can run directly on ARM Cortex-M or Raspberry Pi microcontrollers without needing cloud network bandwidth.

Clinical Explainability

Unlike black-box deep learning (Umer 2022), SHAP values provide clear explanations to clinicians for why risk alerts are generated, building clinical trust.

Global Feature Importance (SHAP Analysis)

Clinical Feature Interpretations

  • time (Follow-up period): Strongest predictor of mortality risk. Shorter follow-up periods without recovery indicate critical post-discharge vulnerability.
  • serum_creatinine: Higher blood levels indicate impaired kidney function, strongly correlated with left ventricular failure.
  • ejection_fraction: Percentage of blood pumped per stroke. Values < 30% indicate severe heart failure.
  • serum_sodium: Hyponatremia (< 135 mEq/L) signals fluid retention and neurohormonal activation.
  • age: Advanced age increases overall vulnerability to heart failure mortality.
